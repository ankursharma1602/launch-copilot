# Positioning (Obviously Awesome method)

Positioning is context that helps customers understand what a product is, why it is special and why they should care. Build it from the evidence, in this order. Each step feeds the next. Do not start from a conclusion.

There is no fill-in-the-blank positioning statement in this output. The six parts are the positioning.

## Method
1. **Competitive alternatives.** What would the best-fit customer do if this did not exist? Include doing nothing, doing it by hand, native tools and other vendors. These are what customers compare against, not only direct competitors. For each, say why people choose it and where it stops. Start with what closed beta customers said they compared it to or used before (`02-launch-context.md`, feedback section). Sources: that feedback, `context/company.md`, the closed beta handoff, `02-launch-context.md`. Anything else is `[NEED PROOF: ...]`. A customer's comparison is how they see the alternatives, not a verified claim about a competitor.
2. **Unique attributes.** Capabilities the alternatives lack, taken from the closed beta handoff. Only attributes you can source. If you cannot verify the comparison, say the attribute is "not yet confirmed as unique".
3. **Value and proof.** For each unique attribute, what does it let the customer do that matters to them? Say it in the words closed beta customers used. Then the proof: an approved proof point, a closed beta handoff fact, or `[NEED PROOF: ...]`.
4. **Best-fit customers.** Who cares most about that value, and what do they look like? Use `03-audience.md` and the closed beta feedback on who got the most value, and refine it from steps 1 to 3.
5. **Market category.** The frame of reference that makes the value obvious. Consider two or three options, choose one, and say what it makes customers assume (good and bad). Do not coin a category with no basis.
6. **Relevant trends.** Trends that make the category matter now. Use only trends in `context/company.md` or the closed beta handoff. Do not invent market data.

Closed beta feedback is a small internal sample and may be labeled mock. It shapes the analysis. It is not approved proof. Any beta number or customer quote used outside the team is `[NEED PROOF: approval to use closed beta results or quotes]`.

Then write what this means for messaging: the three or four ideas messaging must carry. This is a handoff, not a statement.
