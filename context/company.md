## What Clio is
Clio sells legal practice software. The products named in public sources are:
- **Clio Manage:** practice management, including matters, activities, time entries and bills.
- **Clio Grow:** client intake and lead management. Clio's ClioCon 2026 recap says Grow AI captures and screens new leads for fit and conflicts, books consultations and follows up when leads go quiet.
- **Clio Work:** named in the ClioCon 2026 coverage. Drafting in Clio Work was announced there.
- **Clio Phones:** announced at ClioCon 2026 as an add-on across Manage and Grow.
- **Clio Desktop App** and **Clio mobile app:** the Clio help article for Timekeeping uses both as capture sources.
- **Clio Timekeeping:** AI-powered feature that captures billable activity and drafts time entries for the lawyer to review (see below).

## ClioCon 2026 context (Boston, Oct 26-27, 2026)
Source: LawSites wrap-up by Bob Ambrogi, Oct 26, 2026, and Clio's own ClioCon 2026 recap.
- **Clio Timekeeping:** an opt-in beta. It tracks activity as lawyers work, in Clio and in other applications, and prepares matter-matched time entries for review.
- **Unified interface:** Clio Grow, Clio Manage and Clio Work merge into one interface with one login, including a unified inbox for email and texts. Rollout starts in November 2026.
- **Cross-platform AI assistant:** users ask questions or tell Clio to take actions from anywhere in the new interface, using the data they are allowed to access.
- **Benchmarking and Insights:** compares billable hours, utilization, realization and collection rates against aggregated, anonymized Clio data.
- **Hybrid billing:** one invoice can combine a flat fee for part of a matter with hourly billing for the rest.
- **Judiciary-Grade AI:** AI built to meet court standards, starting with the acquisition of Learned Hand (Clio recap).

## Legal AI positioning (as stated publicly)
- The AI is built into the practice management system, so it works on the client, matter, document, communication, deadline, research, time and billing data the user can already access.
- The lawyer reviews and approves. Timekeeping drafts entries. It does not bill on its own.
- Clio describes AI built to meet court standards ("Judiciary-Grade AI").
