# Brand: Clio

> Source: the live clio.com home page, read on Nov 9 2026 (computed styles and section colours from the page itself). This is the rendered site, not Clio's brand guide. Confirm with Clio's brand team before anything goes outside the team.

## The look in one line
Editorial and calm. Very large, ultra-thin headlines with tight letter spacing. Small monospace capitals as section labels. Black is the signature colour: it carries the hero, navigation, footer and every launch asset. White carries long content. Soft tints separate sections. One strong blue for action. Lots of white space.

Rules:
- Black is the main brand colour. Every launch visual (email header, LinkedIn image, banner) is on black with white type. Long-read material (persona table, one-pager body) uses a white canvas with ink text, and opens with a black header band so it still reads as Clio.
- Deep navy and Clio blue are for one strong band or button, not whole designs.
- Tints (pale blue, stone, warm off-white, lavender, sand, olive) are section and card backgrounds, never text colours.
- Clio blue is the single action colour: buttons, toggles, key highlights.
- Persona or category colour coding: use pale blue, stone, lavender and sand as header fills with ink text.
- Contrast: ink on any tint passes. White on Clio blue, deep navy, royal navy and black passes. Never put grey body text on a tint darker than off-white.

## Typeface
- Clio's site uses **Facility** (a commercial sans) for everything, headlines at weight 100 with tight tracking (about -3%), and **Openinto Mono** for small capital labels.
- We don't have those fonts. Use **Inter** (free, and also loaded on clio.com) as the stand-in: headlines Inter 200 or 300 with letter spacing about -0.03em, body Inter 400. Labels: a monospace (`"SF Mono", Menlo, monospace`), small, all capitals, letter spacing about 0.08em.
- Briefs say the typeface is a stand-in for Facility.

## Logo and wordmark
- If an official Clio logo file exists at `assets/clio-logo.svg` (or `.png`), added by the repo owner, place it top left on every visual, unaltered, with clear space. Never redraw, trace, recolour or distort it.
- If no file exists, set the word **Clio** as live text in the headline face, sentence case, white on dark or ink on light, top left with clear space.

## Layout habits to copy
- A small monospace capital label above every headline ("CLIO TIMEKEEPING", "WHY IT MATTERS").
- Headline much larger than anything else, thin weight, short line.
- One or two sentences of supporting text in grey, then one Clio blue button.
- Cards on off-white or a tint, generous padding, small radius, no shadows or gradients.
- Product visuals are simple, clean UI cards (a time entry with a matter and a Bill-ready toggle), drawn in the palette. No stock photos, no copied imagery.

## Launch and social assets (from Clio's own launch post for the 2026 Legal Trends Report, X, Oct 26 2026)
This is the pattern for email headers, LinkedIn images and banners. Minimal: one idea, almost nothing else.
- Black canvas.
- A small white pill at the top centre with a monospace capital label in ink, for example "JUST DROPPED" or "NOW AVAILABLE".
- Below it, a thin headline in white, centred, two short lines at most, tight letter spacing. No supporting paragraph on the image.
- One single, simple abstract object in the lower half, softly lit, in warm tones (sand, orange, cream), for example a curled sheet of paper on a warm surface. One object, no icons, no UI clutter. A soft shadow on that object is fine.
- No buttons, no lists, no feature bullets, no wordmark crowding the headline. The post text carries the detail.
- In-app banners and one-pagers may use the light editorial style above (white canvas, thin ink headline, mono label, one Clio blue button) with at most one clean UI card.

## Never
- Busy layouts. If an image has more than a label, a headline and one object or card, cut it.
- Gradients, glow, emoji, clip art. (The only shadow allowed is the soft light on the single abstract object in launch assets.)
- Any colour not in the table.
