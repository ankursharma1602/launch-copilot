# Voice and style rules

> Tone notes are drawn from the wording of Clio's public help article and ClioCon coverage, which read plain and task-focused. `[NEED PROOF: confirm against Clio's brand voice guide]`.

## Voice
- **Plain and practical.** Say what the feature does and what the lawyer does next. The help article names the exact buttons: "My time entries," "Bill-ready."
- **Lawyer-literate.** Use the words lawyers and billing staff use: matters, time entries, bills, flat fee, hourly, realization.
- **Respectful of the lawyer's judgment.** The lawyer reviews and approves. AI drafts. Say it that way.
- **Short sentences.** One idea per sentence. No jargon we can't explain in one line.
- **Confident, not loud.** State what the product does. Let proof do the persuading.

## Hard rules
- No em dashes. Use commas, colons or a new sentence.
- No exclamation marks in customer-facing copy.
- No invented numbers, customers, quotes or dates. Use `[NEED PROOF: ...]`.
- Never say the AI bills, decides or submits for the lawyer. It drafts entries for review.
- Never imply lawyers are careless about time. The point is that work is hard to capture while doing it.
- Announce Timekeeping as the handoff states it: generally available on launch day, with the date, plans and regions the handoff gives (illustrative). Claim nothing the handoff and `context/` do not support.
- Headlines say what the customer gets, not what the feature is called.

## Banned words and phrases
peace of mind, game-changer, revolutionary, seamless, cutting-edge, best-in-class, unlock, supercharge, leverage (as a verb), robust, effortless, world-class, next-generation, synergy, disrupt

## Preferred patterns
- Problem, then capability, then proof. In that order.
- Lead with the moment the reader recognizes (the hour of work that never made it onto a bill), then show the way through.
- Name the review step every time: the lawyer checks and approves each entry.
